# Case Ports: Layouts of Real Multi-Terminal Seaports (for designing one compact fictional port)

Research date: 2026-11-23. About 17 tool calls. Source quality: Wikipedia, port authority and operator pages, JICA planning reports and trade press. Detailed per-terminal layout data was hard to get from official pages: several fetches returned 404s or empty pages (luka-kp.si, the DLCA Priok page, the Busan New Port wiki).

Every item under "Cited Findings" has a source. Items under "Inferences" marked **[BG]** are general background knowledge or descriptions of how the port looks on satellite imagery or maps. They were NOT verified with a source in this session, and the writer should treat them as plausible but unconfirmed.

## Tanjung Priok (Jakarta): basins, terminals, breakwaters, channel, city and toll links

### Takeaway
Tanjung Priok is a 1,028 ha breakwater-sheltered coastal port, roughly 604 ha water and 424 ha land; two sources give the split the other way round. It has 76 berths, 16.9 km of quay and about 20 terminals of every type. It grew eastward along the shore: the old basins and the passenger and inter-island quays in the west, the container terminals (JICT, Koja) in the middle-east, and the offshore reclaimed New Priok/Kalibaru (NPCT1) further east, with a deeper channel. The city wraps tightly around the landward side, and toll roads run along the port's southern edge.

### Cited Findings
- Total port size 1,028 ha (10.28 km²): water 604 ha, land 424 ha. 76 berths, quay length 16,853 m, total storage area 661,822 m². Harbour type: "coastal breakwater". — [Wikipedia: Port of Tanjung Priok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Tanjung_Priok)
- The Indonesian Wikipedia reverses the figures (water 424 ha, land 604 ha). — [id.wikipedia: Pelabuhan Tanjung Priok](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelabuhan_Tanjung_Priok). **Conflict:** the two Wikipedia editions disagree on which figure is water and which is land.
- About 20 terminals: general cargo, multipurpose, scrap, passenger, dry bulk, liquid bulk, oil, chemicals and three container terminals. JICT (Hutchison + Pelindo) is the largest container terminal in Indonesia. — [Wikipedia EN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Tanjung_Priok)
- The Indonesian Wikipedia lists five container terminals: JICT I, JICT II, TPK Koja, Mustika Alam Lestari (MAL) and Multi Terminal Indonesia (MTI). — [id.wikipedia](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelabuhan_Tanjung_Priok)
- NPCT1 (New Priok Terminal 1, Kalibaru): 32 ha of land, 450 m quay, 16 m draft, ships up to 15,000 TEU, 8 cranes. Joint venture of Pelindo II with a Japan-Singapore consortium (Mitsui among others) on a 25-year concession. — [Wikipedia EN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Tanjung_Priok); [Mitsui NPCT1](https://www.mitsui.com/jp/en/innovation/business/npct1/index.html?ad=id)
- Kalibaru (New Priok) expansion plan: capacity rising from 5 to 18 million TEU/yr, designed for 18,000 TEU ships with a 300 m-wide two-way channel. The full-operation date (2023) is flagged as outdated. Phase 1 was completed in 2016. — [Wikipedia EN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Tanjung_Priok)
- The Kalibaru approach channel and turning basin are designed to −16.5 m LWS. — [ITB journal](https://journals.itb.ac.id/index.php/jts/article/view/18831/6181)
- Throughput was 6.2 M TEU (2016), 6.92 M (2017) and 7.8 M (2018); total capacity about 8 M TEU. — [Wikipedia EN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Tanjung_Priok)
- JICA master-plan studies (older, pre-2020):
  - a long-term plan to open an "east gate" and channel at −14 m;
  - a turning basin in front of JICT/Koja 560 m in diameter (2 × 280 m);
  - moving the breakwater offshore as the channel and basin are widened;
  - a required 740 m separation between the existing JICT/Koja berths and new offshore berths.
  — [JICA report](https://openjicareport.jica.go.jp/pdf/12068599_04.pdf)
- JICA planned the automobile terminal "at the east end of the Tanjung Priok port near the Koja Container Terminal". — [JICA report](https://openjicareport.jica.go.jp/pdf/11744265_02.pdf)
- IPC Car Terminal (IPCC) area and capacity:
  - 7.38 ha / 200,000 vehicles/yr at the start;
  - 31 ha / 700,000 vehicles/yr (2012–2018);
  - after the spin-off, 16.9 ha / 400,000 vehicles/yr.
  — [IPCC presentation 2018](https://www.transportevents.com/presentations/johor2018/IPC_CarTerminal.pdf)
  - A 2022 advertorial says up to 31 ha and more than 1 million vehicles. — [Jakarta Post 2022](https://www.thejakartapost.com/adv/2022/06/07/ipcc-levels-up-as-its-hit-10-year-milestone). **Conflict / likely promotional.**
- Marunda bulk terminal (PT Karya Citra Nusantara), just east of Priok:
  - Pier 1 has operated since 2018;
  - plan: 3 piers totalling 5,350 m, a 100 ha support area, 30–35 Mt/yr of bulk.
  — [Wikipedia EN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Tanjung_Priok)
- City and roads:
  - the Tanjung Priok district hosts the western part of the harbour; the port spans Tanjung Priok and Koja districts;
  - the Ir. Wiyoto Wiyono toll road (Jakarta Inner Ring Road) and the Sunter canal bound the district to the east, with Jakarta Bay to the north;
  - the port dates from 1877, with a rail link via Tanjung Priuk Station (1914).
  — [Wikipedia: Tanjung Priok district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanjung_Priok)
- Transjakarta BRT corridors 10/12 and the KAI Commuter line reach Tanjung Priok station. — [id.wikipedia](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelabuhan_Tanjung_Priok)

### Inferences
- **[BG] West to east sequence.** The old harbour is a row of parallel finger basins opening north into one sheltered outer harbour behind a western and an eastern breakwater. From west to east:
  - Pelabuhan Nusantara (inter-island and Pelni passenger quays, plus small craft);
  - Basin I, then Basin II (general cargo, multipurpose, dry bulk such as grain for the flour mills, and the passenger terminal);
  - Basin III (multipurpose and container terminals);
  - JICT and Koja on the eastern shore;
  - the car terminal near Koja;
  - Kalibaru/NPCT1, built on reclaimed land offshore further east with its own deeper access.
  Oil and liquid-bulk jetties sit in their own segment, and the large Pertamina Plumpang fuel depot lies inland, south of the port. This was not verified this session; exact basin assignments need Pelindo maps.
- **[BG] Road links.** Toll access comes from the south. The Jakarta Harbour Road / Wiyoto Wiyono elevated toll road runs along the southern port edge, and the Cilincing–Cibitung toll road and the New Priok Eastern Access (NPEA) serve Kalibaru. No source on NPEA was found.
- **Planning logic.** The port uses an "eastward ribbon". The city already presses on the west and south, so each newer, deeper terminal goes further east and further offshore on reclaimed land. Newer terminals get deeper water (−14 m old channel vs −16.5 m Kalibaru) and larger ships.
- **Simulator relevance.** Priok shows the typical "old city basins + new offshore container island" pattern. Passengers and inter-island traffic stay nearest the old town.

### Gaps
- No official current Pelindo layout drawing was found, so the exact basin numbering and which terminal sits in which basin are unconfirmed.
- Breakwater lengths and the main channel's current depth and width are not confirmed. (−14 m is a JICA plan figure.)
- The position and capacity of the oil and chemical jetties were not found; nor were those of the grain and coal berths or the ferry and Ro-Ro berths.
- No source on the NPEA toll road was found.

## Port of Rotterdam: separation of container, oil, LNG, dry bulk and chemicals along the river and on reclaimed land

### Takeaway
Rotterdam is a linear port about 40+ km long, about 12,500 ha in total, with more than 6,000 ha of industrial sites. It developed historically from the city centre westward to the sea. The newest, deepest, largest-ship functions are at the seaward tip on reclaimed land: Maasvlakte 1 (1960s–70s) and Maasvlakte 2 (2008–2013, about 2,000 ha gross, 1,000 ha net). These include the mega container terminals, the EMO dry-bulk terminal and the Gate LNG terminal near the entrance. Older chemical and refinery clusters (Botlek, Pernis, Europoort) sit mid-river, and old city harbours have been converted to urban uses.

### Cited Findings
- About 105 km² and about 40 km long. The infobox gives 4,810 ha of harbour + 7,903 ha of land = 12,713 ha. **Conflict noted** within the article (105 vs 127 km²). — [Wikipedia: Port of Rotterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Rotterdam)
- The port authority gives over 40 km in length; 12,500 ha of land and water, of which more than 6,000 ha are industrial sites; about 428 Mt/yr of throughput; 323.2 Mt in the first nine months of 2026. — [Port of Rotterdam facts & figures](https://www.portofrotterdam.com/en/experience-online/facts-and-figures)
- Areas from city to sea:
  1. historic city harbours (Oude Haven, Leuvehaven, now cultural and nightlife);
  2. Maashaven, Rijnhaven and Feijenoord;
  3. Waalhaven, Vondelingenplaat and Eemhaven;
  4. Botlek;
  5. Europoort along the Calandkanaal and Nieuwe Waterweg (rail and refineries);
  6. Maasvlakte on reclaimed land projecting into the North Sea.

  Large oil refineries lie west of the city. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Rotterdam)
- ECT operates the Delta and Euromax container terminals; Delta uses unmanned vehicles. The EECV bulk quay has a 24 m draft for the largest ore carriers, which reach it via the Eurogeul. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Rotterdam)
- Maasvlakte 1 was reclaimed in the 1960s. EMO (the largest bulk terminal in western Europe, for coal and ore) started there in 1973 at the Mississippihaven and took over the Amazonhaven deep-water terminal in 1990. Maasvlakte 1 also holds:
  - the Uniper coal-fired plant (1,040 MW, 1988);
  - the Rotterdam Distribution Centre (DHL, Kloosterboer);
  - the Slufter, a disposal pit for contaminated dredged sludge.
  — [Wikipedia: Maasvlakte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maasvlakte)
- Maasvlakte 2 was built September 2008 – May 2013: about 2,000 ha in total, 1,000 ha net industrial land "on deep water". — [Port of Rotterdam: new port area at sea](https://portofrotterdam.com/en/new-port-area-at-sea); [Government.nl](https://www.government.nl/themes/transport/maritime-transport-and-seaports/development-of-rotterdam-hub-port)
- APM Terminals Maasvlakte II covers 86 ha and has operated since April 2015. Rotterdam World Gateway (DP World-led) is the other Maasvlakte 2 terminal. — [APM Terminals](https://www.apmterminals.com/en/maasvlakte/about/our-terminal); [Wikipedia: Maasvlakte 2](https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/Maasvlakte_2)
- Gate LNG terminal (Gasunie + Vopak):
  - a 35 ha site on Maasvlakte, near the port entrance;
  - originally 3 × 180,000 m³ tanks and 12 bcm/yr, later 16 bcm/yr;
  - a 4th 180,000 m³ tank brings capacity to 20 bcm/yr, reported commissioned around October 2026.
  — [Port of Rotterdam LNG terminal](https://www.portofrotterdam.com/en/lng-terminal); [Gate facts & figures](https://www.gateterminal.com/en/gate-terminal/profiel/facts-figures/); [Hydrocarbon Engineering 2026](https://www.hydrocarbonengineering.com/tanks-terminals/02102026/fourth-lng-tank-at-gate-terminal-ready/)

### Inferences
- **[BG] Separation of traffic.** The port entrance (Maasmond) splits seagoing traffic into the Nieuwe Waterweg (towards the city) and the Calandkanaal/Beerkanaal (towards Europoort and Maasvlakte). A long dam (the Beerdam) separates the two waterways, so tankers and bulk carriers to Europoort do not mix with inland traffic heading for the city.
- **[BG] Maasvlakte 1 zoning:**
  - container terminals (ECT Delta, Euromax, APMT) on the Europahaven and Amazonehaven;
  - EMO dry bulk on the Mississippihaven, on the southern side, separated from the containers;
  - the Maasvlakte Olie Terminal crude-oil tank farm;
  - Gate LNG at the very entrance (Yangtzekanaal area), so LNG carriers spend the least time inside the port.

  The Yangtzehaven location could not be confirmed by search.
- **[BG] Maasvlakte 2** sits seaward of Maasvlakte 1, around the Prinses Amaliahaven, behind a hard seawall plus soft (dune/beach) sea defences. It is almost entirely container terminals with distribution parks behind them. Botlek (chemicals) and Pernis (Shell refinery) sit about 15–25 km inland on the river, between the city and Europoort.
- **Planning logic.** Rotterdam uses a "migration to the sea": each generation of bigger ships pushed the deep-water terminals seaward, and the city reclaimed the old inner harbours. Hazardous energy terminals (LNG, crude oil) sit at or near the entrance and away from the city. Containers occupy the newest deep-water land with large backland and rail/barge links. Dry bulk is clustered separately (EMO) next to the power plants that consume it.
- **Distance.** At "over 40 km" in length, Maasvlakte is roughly 40 km from the city centre (inferred from the port length).

### Gaps
- Official per-area hectares for Botlek, Europoort and Maasvlakte 1 were not obtained.
- The exact harbour basin assigned to each terminal was not confirmed by a source (Gate on the Yangtzekanaal, Maasvlakte Olie Terminal location, and so on).
- The 2025 throughput breakdown by cargo type was not retrieved.

## Singapore: Tanjong Pagar, Pasir Panjang, Tuas, Jurong Island and the cruise centres

### Takeaway
Singapore is consolidating all container handling from the city-centre terminals (Tanjong Pagar, Keppel, Brani) and Pasir Panjang into one mega port at Tuas at the far western end of the island. Tuas will have 1,337 ha and 65 M TEU when completed in the 2040s. This frees about 1,000 ha of waterfront for the "Greater Southern Waterfront" city. Petrochemicals are segregated on Jurong Island, about 3,000–3,500 ha reclaimed by merging seven islets, between Pasir Panjang and Tuas. Cruise terminals stay in the city (HarbourFront and Marina South).

### Cited Findings
- Tuas Port: about 1,337 ha and 65 M TEU when all phases complete in the 2040s. Phase 1 reclamation was completed in November 2021; Phase 2 reclamation began in March 2018 and includes 9.1 km of caissons. — [MPA: Port of the Future](https://mpa.gov.sg/maritime-singapore/port-of-the-future); [MOT speech 2021](https://www.mot.gov.sg/news-resources/newsroom/remarks-by-minister-for-transport-mr-s-iswaran-at-the-mpa-25th-anniversary-completion-of-tuas-port-phase-1-reclamation-event/)
- PSA is moving the Keppel and Brani city terminals to Tuas by 2027 (when the leases expire), and Pasir Panjang to Tuas by 2040. Tanjong Pagar was vacated earlier (reported as 2017), with ancillary use. The relocation frees about 1,000 ha for the Southern Waterfront City. — [Port Economics Management: Tuas](https://porteconomicsmanagement.org/pemp/contents/part3/changing-geography-of-seaports/singapore-tuas-port-expansion-project); [CLC Box Story: Port and the City](https://knowledgehub.clc.gov.sg/liveability-in-action/box-story--port-and-the-city-balancing-growth-and-liveability/). Some date details (for example the start of Phase 1) conflict between sources.
- Jurong Island:
  - seven south-western islets (total 991 ha) were merged by reclamation from 1995 to 25 September 2009;
  - reported size ranges from about 3,000 ha to over 3,500 ha;
  - it is a dedicated chemicals/petrochemicals island.
  — [Wikipedia: Jurong Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurong_Island); [NLB Infopedia](https://eresources.nlb.gov.sg/infopedia/articles/SIP_1076_2010-05-14.html); [MTI speech 2009](https://www.mti.gov.sg/newsroom/mr-lim-hng-kiang-at-the-jurong-island-reclamation-completion-ceremony--25-sep-09/)
- The CLC Urban Systems Study on Jurong Island covers the planning rationale for an isolated chemical cluster. — [CLC USS Jurong Island](https://clc.gov.sg/docs/default-source/urban-systems-studies/uss-jurong-island)

### Inferences
- **[BG] East to west sequence along the south coast:**
  - Marina Bay Cruise Centre (Marina South, opened 2012) and the CBD;
  - Tanjong Pagar, Keppel and Brani (city container terminals, now being vacated);
  - HarbourFront / Singapore Cruise Centre (ferries and regional cruise, next to Sentosa);
  - Pasir Panjang Terminal (about 10 km west of the CBD);
  - Jurong Port (multipurpose and bulk: cement, steel) and the Jurong Island petrochemical complex, linked to the mainland by a single causeway with controlled access;
  - Tuas mega port at the far western tip, about 40+ km from the CBD and next to the Tuas industrial estate and the Second Link to Malaysia.

  Anchorages and bunkering areas lie offshore in the Singapore Strait.
- **Planning logic.** Passengers stay in the city (cruise centres in the CBD and on the waterfront). Containers move to the newest, largest reclaimed deep-water land away from the city. Hazardous petrochemicals go on a separate island with restricted access, buffered from homes by water. The mega container port gets its own dedicated backland and automation.

### Gaps
- No sourced figures were obtained for the Marina Bay Cruise Centre location and size, or for the Singapore Cruise Centre.
- No sourced current berth counts or depths for Pasir Panjang or Tuas Phase 1. (Commonly cited: Tuas berths at −23 m and about 21 berths in Phase 1; unverified.)
- The exact distances CBD → Pasir Panjang → Tuas were not sourced.

## Busan New Port (chosen instead of Jebel Ali) and a Jebel Ali note

### Takeaway
Busan split its port in two: the old city-centre North Port (now being redeveloped, partly as a passenger and ferry hub) and a purpose-built Busan New Port about 25–30 km west on the Busan/Jinhae boundary, which handles most containers. In 2024 New Port handled about 17.9 M TEU against North Port's 6.5 M. Jinhae New Port is planned next to it, aiming at about 39.7 M TEU combined by about 2045. Jebel Ali is a huge (about 135 km² by one figure) man-made harbour 35 km south-west of Dubai, attached to the JAFZA free zone.

### Cited Findings
- Busan New Port is planned to have 53 berths and 35 M TEU/yr capacity; another city page says 51 berths. Full completion is given as 2037, or 2040 on a different page. — [Busan City: Busan Port](https://www.busan.go.kr/eng/bsport); [Busan City: Business](https://busan.go.kr/eng/Business). **Conflict** on berth count and completion year.
- Throughput in 2024: New Port about 17.9 M TEU, North Port about 6.5 M TEU, total about 24.4 M TEU. — [Busan City: yearly results](https://www.busan.go.kr/eng/container-processing)
- Jinhae New Port: about USD 10 bn, 66 berths across Busan and Jinhae, a combined target of 39.66 M TEU by 2045. Phase 1 has 9 berths under a single operator and more than 6 M TEU/yr. — [Lloyd's List](https://www.lloydslist.com/LL1151802/Busan-to-undergo-$10bn-transformation-into-megaport); [Maritime Executive](https://www.maritime-executive.com/article/south-korea-to-invest-10b-to-expand-busan-port-s-competitiveness); [Kuehne+Nagel](https://mykn.kuehne-nagel.com/news/article/busan-to-undergo-usd-10bn-transformation-into-13-Dec-2024)
- Busan launched its first automated container terminal at New Port. — [Maritime Executive](https://maritime-executive.com/article/south-korea-s-busan-port-launches-first-automated-container-terminal)
- Jebel Ali has 67 berths and covers about 134.68 km² (described as the largest man-made harbour; the figure likely includes the wider port and zone area). It has more than 1 million m² of container yard and 960,000 m² of open storage, lies 35 km south-west of Dubai and opened on 26 February 1979. Throughput was 13.7 M TEU in 2021. — [Wikipedia: Port of Jebel Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Jebel_Ali)

### Inferences
- **[BG] Busan New Port layout.** It sits in a sheltered bay with container piers on both the north and south sides (North Container Terminal piers 1–4 on the north shore, South Container Terminals and the west terminals on reclaimed islands). Large logistics complexes (Ungdong, North/South hinterland) lie directly behind. The old North Port near downtown has kept the international passenger terminal (Busan Port International Passenger Terminal) and is being redeveloped as a waterfront.
- **[BG] Jebel Ali layout.** A rectangular dredged harbour protected by a long offshore breakwater, with container Terminals 1–4 along the quay walls. Ro-Ro, bulk and general cargo occupy separate quays. The JAFZA free zone lies immediately inland, and the oil and petrochemical jetties are segregated.
- **Planning logic.** Both are greenfield mega container ports built away from the old city port. They have huge contiguous backland (free zone or logistics park), while passengers remain at the city port.

### Gaps
- No per-terminal quay lengths or depths were obtained for Busan New Port; the Wikipedia page returned 404. A [PSA Busan terminals PDF](https://www.globalpsa.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/BUSAN-TERMINALS.pdf) exists but was not fetched.
- Jebel Ali breakwater, depth, terminal-by-terminal layout and the cargo zoning beyond containers are not sourced.

## Compact medium port: Port of Koper (Slovenia), the closest to a simulator map

### Takeaway
Koper fits 12 specialised terminals into only about 280 ha, with 30 berths and a maximum depth of 18 m. The port sits right next to a historic town. That makes it a strong template for a compact multi-cargo simulator map: several basins side by side, each specialised, a passenger terminal on the town waterfront and bulk/energy on the far side.

### Cited Findings
- Port of Koper: 280 ha, 30 berths, 2 piers, maximum depth 18 m, on the Gulf of Koper in the northern Adriatic. — [Wikipedia: Port of Koper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Koper)
- 12 terminals: container & Ro-Ro; car; general cargo; fruit; timber; minerals; cereals & fodder; alumina; European Energy (coal/iron ore); liquid cargoes; livestock; passenger. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Koper)
- Development began in 1957 on the northern shore of Koper (135 m initially) with a dyke across the Stanjon inlet. A 31 km rail line to Prešnica opened in 1967. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Koper)

### Inferences
- **[BG] Basin layout.** Koper's port has three parallel basins (Basin I, II, III) cut into reclaimed land north and north-east of the old town, opening west to the gulf:
  - Basin I is the main general/container area, with the container terminal on Pier I;
  - car terminals and large car parks occupy much of the land between the basins, including multi-storey car garages;
  - Basin II and the outer areas hold the European Energy Terminal (coal and iron ore, at the outer, seaward end with open stockpiles) and dry bulk;
  - the liquid-cargo tank farm sits separately on the outer and eastern edge;
  - the passenger (cruise) terminal is on the old-town quay, outside the cargo port, within walking distance of the centre.

  This needs checking against the official Luka Koper map; the luka-kp.si fetch failed.
- **[BG] Expansion.** Luka Koper has been extending Pier I (container terminal) and building additional car-storage garages, and has a Basin III plan. Status is unverified.
- **Simulator relevance.** Koper's density (about 43 berths per 1,000 ha equivalent; 280 ha for 12 cargo types) and the town–port adjacency are close to what a single-screen simulator map needs. Patimban (Indonesia), with car terminal plus container on reclaimed land, and Tanjung Perak (Surabaya) were not researched in this session.

### Gaps
- Official terminal areas, quay lengths, positions and capacities (luka-kp.si returned 404).
- Koper's recent throughput and the Pier I extension status.
- Tanjung Perak, Patimban and Gdańsk were not researched due to the tool budget.

## Common layout patterns and principles for a compact simulator map

### Takeaway
Across all cases, there is a consistent spatial logic:
1. The old port and the passengers stay at the city.
2. The newest, deepest container terminals go seaward or offshore on reclaimed land with large rectangular backland and road/rail links.
3. Hazardous energy (LNG, crude, chemicals) is placed near the entrance or on a separate island or peninsula, buffered from homes.
4. Dry bulk is its own cluster (dusty, ideally downwind and next to its consuming industry).
5. Breakwaters shelter one outer harbour from which specialised finger basins branch.

### Cited Findings
- Rotterdam: LNG (Gate) is "near the port entrance" on Maasvlakte. Containers and deep-sea bulk (EMO, 24 m EECV quay) are on the newest reclaimed land at the sea end, while the old city harbours became cultural and nightlife areas. — [Port of Rotterdam LNG](https://www.portofrotterdam.com/en/lng-terminal); [Wikipedia Rotterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Rotterdam)
- Singapore: containers are moving from the city terminals to Tuas (1,337 ha), freeing about 1,000 ha of city waterfront. Petrochemicals are concentrated on a separate reclaimed island (Jurong Island). — [MPA](https://mpa.gov.sg/maritime-singapore/port-of-the-future); [CLC](https://knowledgehub.clc.gov.sg/liveability-in-action/box-story--port-and-the-city-balancing-growth-and-liveability/); [Wikipedia Jurong Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurong_Island)
- Tanjung Priok: the newest terminal (NPCT1/Kalibaru) is on offshore reclaimed land with a deeper channel (−16.5 m) and needs a 740 m separation from the older JICT/Koja berths. The car terminal is placed next to the container terminals at the east end. — [ITB](https://journals.itb.ac.id/index.php/jts/article/view/18831/6181); [JICA](https://openjicareport.jica.go.jp/pdf/12068599_04.pdf); [JICA](https://openjicareport.jica.go.jp/pdf/11744265_02.pdf)
- Busan: a new purpose-built container port (17.9 M TEU) is separate from the old city port (6.5 M TEU). — [Busan City](https://www.busan.go.kr/eng/container-processing)
- Koper: 12 terminal types in 280 ha, showing the feasible density for a medium port. — [Wikipedia Koper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Koper)

### Inferences (design principles for one compact fictional port)
1. **Sequence from the sea inward.** The order runs: sea, then the breakwater with one entrance, then a turning basin, then the terminals.

   *Terminals at or just inside the entrance:*
   - **Hazardous liquids** (LNG, crude, chemicals) on their own jetty, arm or island, so these ships turn in quickly and never pass other terminals. In Rotterdam Gate LNG is at the entrance; in Singapore the petrochemicals are on Jurong Island.
   - **Deep-water container terminal** on the outermost reclaimed land: straight, long quay (in reality 450 m+ per terminal; NPCT1 has 450 m and 32 ha), a deep berth (−16 m or more) and a large rectangular yard. This is the Kalibaru, Maasvlakte 2 and Tuas pattern.

   *Terminals in the middle zone:*
   - **Dry bulk** (coal, ore, grain): open stockyards with conveyors, placed apart from the container yard and from the city, and ideally downwind of the city (an [BG] environmental-practice assumption, not sourced here). Grain silos can sit closer in, being less dusty, often next to flour mills.
   - **Car / Ro-Ro terminal** next to the container terminal with large flat parking (16.9–31 ha in IPCC's case), and a Ro-Ro ramp berth.
   - **General cargo and multipurpose** in the older finger basins.

   *Terminals at the inner end, near the city:*
   - **Passenger / cruise and ferry terminals** next to the town centre and public transport (Priok station and BRT, Koper old-town quay, Singapore Marina South/HarbourFront, Busan North Port).
   - Small craft, tugs, the port authority office, customs.
2. **Water side.**
   - Use one main breakwater (or two arms) with a single dredged approach channel (about 300 m wide for two-way traffic in the Kalibaru design).
   - Provide a turning basin of about 1.5–2× the design ship length in diameter (JICA's 560 m basin in front of JICT/Koja).
   - Separate the waterways where possible so that tanker and bulk traffic does not cross the passenger and ferry routes (Rotterdam's split between the Calandkanaal and the Nieuwe Waterweg [BG]).
3. **Land side.**
   - Container and bulk terminals need the deepest backland and direct toll, highway and rail links that bypass the city (Priok's toll roads along the southern edge, Rotterdam's A15 and Betuweroute [BG]).
   - Logistics parks and free zones sit behind the container terminal (Rotterdam Distribution Centre, JAFZA, Busan hinterland).
4. **Growth direction.** Leave room for expansion seaward or along the coast away from the city. Every case expanded that way: Priok eastward, Rotterdam westward, Singapore westward and Busan westward.
5. **Compact simulator compromise (Koper-like).** Use 3 to 4 finger basins off one sheltered harbour, in this order from the entrance:
   - basin 1 (outer): energy and liquids on a detached jetty, and the dry-bulk stockyard;
   - basin 2: the container quay and yard, with car storage behind;
   - basin 3: general cargo, multipurpose and grain silos;
   - basin 4 (inner): passengers, ferry and the city waterfront.

   A highway and rail spine runs behind all terminals, with a separate gate per terminal.
