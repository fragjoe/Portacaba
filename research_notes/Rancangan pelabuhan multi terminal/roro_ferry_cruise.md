# Ro-Ro (PCTC), Ro-Pax Ferry, Cruise and Small-Ferry Pontoon Terminals: Berths, Ramps, Equipment, Buildings, Layout

Context: the simulator already has a 200 m PCTC (stern quarter ramp), a 120 m Ro-Pax ferry (stern ramp) and a 230 m passenger ship. None of their terminal facilities exist yet. The "BUILD LIST" items in each Inferences section mark what has to be modelled.

## Ro-Ro / PCTC terminal: quay form, ramp loads, shore ramps, yards, PDI, tractors + Mafi, berth size

### Takeaway
A PCTC berth is a plain straight quay. The ship's own stern quarter ramp (angled about 30-45 deg, roughly 9-10 m driveway, up to about 300 t design load on modern designs) lands directly on the apron, so no shore ramp or linkspan is needed. The landside is mostly flat open parking with blocks of cars, multi-storey car parks, technical/PDI and wash centres, plus terminal tractors with Mafi roll trailers for high and heavy cargo. Published berth figures for car terminals run from about 220-250 m long at -10 m depth (single berth) up to 460 m at -12 m (two berths).

### Cited Findings
- The stern quarter ramp is the most common configuration on PCTCs and PCCs. On PCTCs it is strengthened for high and heavy cargo (industrial and agricultural equipment, project cargo on trailers). — [Port Economics, Management and Policy: Ramp Configurations](https://porteconomicsmanagement.org/pemp/contents/part5/automotive-roro-markets/ramps-roro-vessels/)
- A quarter ramp is angled, usually offset 30-45 deg from the ship's centreline, and can double as a watertight door when folded. — [Wärtsilä Encyclopedia: car carriers/ramps](https://www.wartsila.com/encyclopedia/term/ramps)
- Example ramp spec, Knud Hansen 8600 CEU dual-fuel PCTC design (2025): stern ramp driveway 10.00 m at the narrowest point, permissible load 300 t. Side ramp 10 t. 13 car decks, of which 5 are movable. — [Knud Hansen 8600 CEU PCTC sheet](https://www.knudehansen.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/8600-CEU-DF-PCTC-22014.pdf)
- Knud Hansen 12200 CEU PCTC: rear quarter ramp driveway width 9.00 m. — [Knud Hansen 12200 CEU PCTC sheet](https://www.knudehansen.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/12200-CEU-PCTC-22014.27-Vers.-D-with-sleipner-logo.pdf)
- A quarter stern ramp is made wider at its ship end so trucks can turn without backing up. The stern door is slightly wider than the ramp. Axle loads of about 65 t (forklifts) and 45 t (trailer trucks) were design values in this dated (1980) source. — [Maritime Reporter 1980](https://magazines.marinelink.com/Magazines/MaritimeReporter/198006/page/14)
- Ro-ro vessels at quays with a large height difference need long ramps. — [Wärtsilä Encyclopedia: ramps](https://www.wartsila.com/encyclopedia/term/ramps)
- Car-terminal storage layouts:
  - 5x4 blocks, where at most one car must be moved to reach any car (long-term storage)
  - diagonal rows, for batch processing of 5-12 vehicles
  - fishbone, for pre-sorting before loading onto truck or rail
  - multi-deck storage, which cuts the footprint at extra cost and is used where land is scarce
  — [PEMP: Storage configurations at car terminals](https://porteconomicsmanagement.org/storage-car-terminals/)
- A private car parking space is about 12.5 m2 net (Hong Kong Transport Dept design manual). — [HK TD manual V7](https://www.td.gov.hk/filemanager/en/content_5055/V7_11_2025.pdf)
- BLG AutoTerminal Bremerhaven:
  - about 295 ha with about 70,000 vehicle spaces (about 30,000 covered, about 40,000 open) and 8 multi-storey car parks
  - 18 car-carrier berths along about 5 km of quay; an older BLG page says 9 berths, and older sources give 240 ha
  - three technical centres doing PDI, de-waxing/de-conservation and washing, described as Europe's largest automobile workshop; nearly 500,000 vehicles processed per year
  — [BLG AutoTerminal Bremerhaven](https://blg-logistics.com/en/autoterminal-bremerhaven); [bremenports automobile](https://www.bremenports.de/en/ports/competences/automobile); [Bremerhaven.de BLG](https://bremerhaven.de/en/economy/harbours-shipyards/blg-logistics-group.27759.html); [WFB "Biggest car park in Europe"](https://wfb-bremen.de/en/page/bremen-invest/investing-in-bremen-automotive-industries-the-biggest-car-park-in-europe-blg-logistic-group-ag)
- IPC Car Terminal (IPCC), Tanjung Priok. The area figures conflict between sources:
  - 7.38 ha and 200,000 vehicles/yr in 2007
  - 16.9 ha and 400,000 vehicles/yr in a 2018 company presentation
  - "up to 31 ha" and more than 1 million vehicles in a 2022 advertorial
  — [IPCC presentation 2018](https://www.transportevents.com/presentations/johor2018/IPC_CarTerminal.pdf); [Jakarta Post 2022](https://www.thejakartapost.com/adv/2022/06/07/ipcc-levels-up-as-its-hit-10-year-milestone)
- JICA studies for Jakarta/Tanjung Priok:
  - car-carrier berth design of -10.0 m depth and 250 m length, one berth
  - a later master plan: imported/exported vehicle berths of 460 m total at -12 m, with 128,000 m2 of storage required; at the time one 220 m berth with a 156,000 m2 yard existed
  - a planned car terminal of about 70,000 m2 (yard, roads, receiving area, office, gate) plus apron, about 85,000 m2 in total
  — [JICA report 11744265_02](https://openjicareport.jica.go.jp/pdf/11744265_02.pdf); [JICA report 12068599_05](https://openjicareport.jica.go.jp/pdf/12068599_05.pdf)
- Mafi roll trailers:
  - standard payloads 40-120 t, tare 7-10 t; the dealer range runs from 1 t to 160 t
  - example (dealer listings, unverified): 16 t unit 5.16 x 2.0 m, height 0.68 m
  - 22 t unit, platform 4.0 x 2.5 m, overall 5.75 m long, height 0.895 m
  - 30 t unit, platform 6.1 x 2.45 m, overall 7.28 x 2.5 m, height 0.75 m, weight 4 t
  - 56 t gooseneck unit, 12.85 x 3.05 x 0.95 m
  - 120 t unit, about 12.2 x 2.59 m platform at 0.73 m deck height, tare about 9.2 t
  — [Roll trailer (Wikiwand)](https://wikiwand.com/en/articles/Roll_trailer); [Morello Mafi](https://www.morello.eu.com/?p=20623); [Machineseeker 30 t](https://www.machineseeker.ie/mafi-6300%2F2500%2Fh750+mm/i-22028363); [Machineseeker 22 t](https://www.machineseeker.com/mafi-4000%2F2500%2Fh895+mm/i-21298637); [Autoline 56 t](https://autoline.com/-/sale/low-loader-trailers/Mafi/56-TON--23042420525651490700); [Exapro 120 t](https://www.exapro.com/seacom-ut40-120t-p260731142/)
- Roll trailers are towed by a powered tractor (terminal tug), sold as a system with Mafi tow equipment. — [Wajax Mafi roll cargo system](https://mh.wajax.com/new-equipment/tow-equipment-utility-vehicles/mafi-roll-cargo-trailer-system/)

### Inferences
- Quay form: build a straight, solid, fendered quay with a wide, flat, unobstructed apron. The quarter ramp, angled about 30-45 deg off the stern, lands on the apron about 30-40 m behind and to the side of the stern, so keep the apron clear of cranes and rails. No linkspan is needed where the quay is level with the ramp at all tides.
- A 200 m PCTC needs about 220-250 m of berth length at roughly -10 to -12 m depth (from the JICA figures).
- Yard density: 12.5 m2 is the net bay. Including aisles, plan roughly 20-25 m2 gross per car (my estimate, not sourced). Bremerhaven's 295 ha / 70,000 cars, about 42 m2 per space, includes all roads, rail and buildings.
- BUILD LIST, PCTC:
  - straight quay with fenders and bollards
  - ramp landing zone with a painted pad
  - open car parking blocks (5x4 blocks, fishbone pre-sort area)
  - multi-storey car park, 4-6 decks with ramps
  - PDI / technical centre shed
  - car wash bay
  - gatehouse and truck loading area
  - car transporter trucks
  - rail car-carrier wagons (optional)
  - terminal tractors and Mafi roll trailers in several sizes
  - drivers' shuttle minibus (drivers drive the cars on and off and need a ride back)
  - light towers, office block

### Gaps
- I found no manufacturer-published MacGregor or TTS quarter ramp length; it is typically about 30-40 m, but unsourced.
- There is no PIANC figure for apron width at PCTC berths. The TU Delft "Ports and Terminals" Ro/Ro chapter was too large to fetch.
- I found no terminal-tractor (Ro-Ro tug) spec. Kalmar and Terberg make Ro-Ro tractors, but they were not verified here.
- No IPCC multi-storey building data was found.

## Ro-Pax ferry terminal: linkspans, double-deck, berth form, walkways, check-in, staging lanes, border control, tide

### Takeaway
A ferry berth is a slot or finger berth with fender walls or dolphins guiding the ship's stern or bow onto an adjustable shore ramp (linkspan). The linkspan is about 25-45 m long and 8-28 m wide (2-3 lanes), and is either floating (pontoon or submerged-tank) or lifted by hydraulics, wires or counterweights. Big ports add a double-deck upper span plus an enclosed passenger walkway. Landside you need passport-control booths, then operator check-in booths, then numbered staging lanes.

### Cited Findings
- A linkspan is a drawbridge-type ramp that adjusts to the ferry's car deck height, mainly to absorb the tide.
  - Types: early hydraulic/cable spans (superseded); train-ferry spans (gradient about 1:25, at least 50 m long for a 2 m tide); "precise fit" Dover berths with flaps of about 2-2.5 m; submerged-tank linkspans (Marine Development, 1970s) where buoyant legs and a counterweight tank carry the outer end, handling up to about 2 m of vertical ship movement; and traditional counterweight/winch/hydraulic/climbing types.
  - Widths range from about 8 m (2 lanes) on early spans to about 28 m on later ones (3-lane lower deck plus 2-lane upper access).
  - Dover has Marine Development "double deck" linkspans that load two decks at once. Stop fenders protect the linkspan from the ship.
  — [Wikipedia: Linkspan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linkspan)
- Where there is a significant tidal range, gradients on a fixed ramp get too steep; linkspans fix this by lifting/lowering or floating. — [Wikipedia: Linkspan](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linkspan)
- Dover Eastern Docks Berth 1 (now demolished): overall 72 m long x 24 m wide measured from the quay face, passenger walkway 1.75 m wide, lift weight including ramps 780 t. — [Dover Ferry Photos: Berth 1](https://www.doverferryphotosforums.co.uk/project-42-number-1-berth-eastern-docks/)
- Dover Eastern Docks has 7 twin-level (double-deck) ferry berths with associated departure buildings. — [Wikipedia: Port of Dover](https://Www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dover_Marina)
- Portsmouth Berth 3 floating double-deck linkspan (2002-03, the only dated project here):
  - lower deck 32 m x 45 m, 700 t, carrying 3 HGV lanes, up to 180 t abnormal loads or double-stacked Mafi trailers
  - upper deck 500 t, 2 HGV lanes, adjustable 2 m vertically relative to the lower deck and 4.5 m horizontally
  - goes from minimum to maximum freeboard in under 15 minutes
  — [Maritime Journal: Portsmouth double deck linkspan](https://www.maritimejournal.com/?p=213177)
- Dover check-in sequence: border/passport control booths (UK exit and French PAF juxtaposed controls), then operator check-in booths, then the driver is sent to an allocated, numbered lane to wait for boarding. Separate operator lanes are used. Coaches get three queuing lanes, and four extra PAF booths were added. — [P&O Ferries blog](https://blog.poferries.com/2019/03/08/what-to-expect-when-you-check-in-at-dover); [DFDS check-in](https://www.dfds.com/en-gb/passenger-ferries/passenger-information/check-in-and-boarding); [Route One: Dover coach](https://www.route-one.net/features/port-of-dover-improvements-bring-mixed-results-for-coach/)
- Ro-Pax vessels typically use bow or stern ramps rather than quarter ramps. — [PEMP: ramps](https://porteconomicsmanagement.org/pemp/contents/part5/automotive-roro-markets/ramps-roro-vessels/)
- Ferry passenger boarding bridges: ADELTE THALASSA (fixed elevation columns, for ferry terminals) and CENTAURUS (single structure, one door, for one ferry type). Kiel Schwedenkai has a rail-mounted moving bridge with 2 m wide tunnels, maximum length 45 m. Ferry tunnels tend to be larger than cruise ones to speed passenger flow. — [ADELTE SPBBs](https://adelte.com/seaports/seaport-passenger-boarding-bridges); [ADELTE Kiel Schwedenkai](https://www.adelte.com/project/kiel-schwedenkai-terminal); [Cruise & Ferry: Bridging the gap](https://cruiseandferry.net/articles/bridging-the-gap-between-ship-and-shore)
- HSE treats linkspans and walkways as distinct port hazards needing segregation of vehicles and pedestrians. — [HSE: Linkspans and walkways](https://www.hse.gov.uk/ports/linkspans-walkways.htm)

### Inferences
- For a 120 m stern-loading Ro-Pax, model the berth as follows:
  - a fendered "slot" berth about 130-150 m long: a fender wall or line of dolphins on one or both sides, and the stern pointing at the quay
  - a single-deck linkspan about 25-35 m long x 15-20 m wide (2-3 lanes)
  - a hinge at the quay end, with a pontoon or hydraulic lift tower at the outer end
  - a short ship-ramp landing flap
- For a 200 m ferry, add an upper-deck span and an elevated passenger walkway or gangway tower. The deck figures come from the Portsmouth and Dover examples; the 130-150 m berth length is my estimate.
- BUILD LIST, Ro-Pax:
  - finger or slot berth with fender panels and breasting/mooring dolphins with catwalks
  - linkspan: hinged deck, lift towers or pontoon, side rails, stop fender
  - optional upper-deck linkspan
  - enclosed passenger walkway on stilts, with a gangway head
  - toll-plaza style check-in booths (4-10, with canopy)
  - border control booths and a secondary inspection shed
  - numbered staging lanes, painted, about 3.5 m wide and 100-200 m long (my assumption)
  - separate freight (HGV/trailer) marshalling and unaccompanied-trailer park
  - passenger terminal building (ticket hall, waiting, café)
  - foot-passenger bus
  - tractors for unaccompanied trailers
  - car, campervan, coach, HGV and motorbike vehicle models

### Gaps
- No current (2015-2026) Dover lane or booth counts or staging-area dimensions are published. The MacGregor/TTS linkspan PDF was unreadable (binary).
- I found no source for Ro-Pax berth lengths for 120-200 m ships. The tidal range at Dover was not retrieved (about 6-7 m spring range is commonly cited, but unverified here).

## Cruise terminal: berth form, boarding bridges, building size/functions, bus/taxi, provisioning, ship sizes

### Takeaway
A cruise berth is a long straight quay or pier, with berth length about LOA + 10% and a turning basin about 2 x LOA. A wide apron carries baggage, provisioning trucks and utilities. One or two rail-mounted mobile passenger boarding bridges per berth reach doors from about 1.1 to 9.5 m above the quay. The terminal building is typically 8,000-20,000 m2 (up to about 45,700 m2 for a mega-terminal), with bag drop, security, check-in, waiting, immigration and baggage reclaim, plus coach, taxi and car parking.

### Cited Findings
- Berth length allows a minimum clearance of 10% of LOA; turning basin diameter is 2 x LOA plus a weather allowance. Fenders run the full frontage, and dolphins secure the offshore end where the pier is shorter than the ship. — [PEMP: Cruise terminal design and equipment](https://porteconomicsmanagement.org/cruise-terminal-design-equipment/)
- Apron uses: line handling, baggage, utilities, waste, supplies, vehicle circulation and parking, emergency access. For large ships in homeport use, two fully automated mobile gangways per berth handle more than 1,000 passengers per hour. — [PEMP](https://porteconomicsmanagement.org/cruise-terminal-design-equipment/)
- Average cruise ship capacity grew from about 1,300 to 3,100 passengers since the 2000s, and average length from about 200 m to over 300 m. — [PEMP](https://porteconomicsmanagement.org/cruise-terminal-design-equipment/)
- Turnaround and utilities:
  - homeport turnaround in under 24 h, preferably 8 h or less; port-of-call transfers usually under 1 h
  - provisioning trucks arrive just-in-time soon after berthing; terminals rarely have warehouses
  - utilities: bunkering, waste reception, potable water, shore power, LNG
  — [PEMP](https://porteconomicsmanagement.org/cruise-terminal-design-equipment/)
- Building functions:
  - embarkation: entrance, bag drop, luggage and passenger X-ray, queuing, ticketing, check-in, waiting, boarding corridors, offices, VIP areas
  - disembarkation: corridors, customs, immigration, quarantine, police, and luggage lay-down (often the largest space), meeting area
  - in multi-storey terminals, disembarkation is mostly on the ground floor and embarkation upstairs
  - ground transport: coach parking, taxi lines, short-stay drop-off, long-stay parking (important in US homeports)
  — [PEMP](https://porteconomicsmanagement.org/cruise-terminal-design-equipment/)
- ADELTE ARGOS gangway: maximum docking height 9.5 m, minimum 1.1 m above the quay. — [ADELTE ARGOS catalogue](https://pdf.nauticexpo.com/pdf/adelte/argos-gangway/61418-81389.html)
- ADELTE cruise bridge types:
  - HYDRA: mobile single structure
  - SEDNA: multiple steel towers, for narrow berths
  - KRONUS: telescopic, fixed at the building end
  - PEGASUS: tunnels perpendicular to the berth
  - designed for a 20-year life; tunnel slopes meet EN/BS/ADA accessibility standards
  - example sites: Barcelona Palacruceros, Boston Black Falcon, Seattle Smith Cove
  — [ADELTE SPBBs](https://adelte.com/seaports/seaport-passenger-boarding-bridges)
- Harwich: a moveable PBB with a total length of 160 m, the largest enclosed passenger access system built for a cruise terminal. — [ADELTE Harwich](https://adelte.com/project/harwich-international-cruise-terminal)
- ADELTE has delivered more than 110 seaport bridges since 1998, with a 20-30 year life. Port Canaveral has 6 HYDRA bridges (2016). — [Cruise & Ferry](https://cruiseandferry.net/articles/bridging-the-gap-between-ship-and-shore)
- TK Elevator also makes seaport boarding bridges. — [TK Elevator seaport boarding bridges](https://airportsolutions.tkelevator.com/global-en/products/seaport-boarding-bridges)
- Terminal sizes:
  - PortMiami MSC terminal (2025): about 45,700 m2, 2 berths (expandable to 3 by 2029), up to 36,000 passenger movements per day, 42 baggage screeners
  - Santa Cruz de Tenerife: 8,400 m2, 5,300 embarking plus 5,300 disembarking at once, 50 check-in desks
  - Southampton: more than 100,000 sq ft (about 9,300 m2), more than 6,000 passengers per day
  - Galataport Istanbul (planned): 20,000 m2 terminal, 100 check-in desks
  — [Dezeen: MSC PortMiami](https://www.dezeen.com/2026/09/16/arquitectonica-msc-cruise-terminal-port-miami/amp/); [Seatrade: Tenerife](https://www.seatrade-cruise.com/ports-destinations/santa-cruz-de-tenerife-presents-europe-s-newest-cruise-terminal); [IDEA StatiCa: Southampton](https://www.ideastatica.com/nl/company/projects/southampton-ship-terminal-united-kingdom); [Seatrade search result](https://www.seatrade-cruise.com/node/31723)

### Inferences
- For the 230 m passenger ship, plan a berth of at least about 255 m (230 x 1.1), with a turning basin of about 460 m diameter, plus mooring dolphins if the pier is shorter.
- A 230 m ship carries about 2,000-3,000 passengers. A Tenerife/Southampton-sized building of about 8,000-10,000 m2, 2 storeys, fits; the passenger count is an estimate.
- BUILD LIST, cruise:
  - long quay with full-length fenders and bollards
  - 1-2 rail-mounted PBBs: tunnel about 2 m wide, door height 1.1-9.5 m, on rails along an elevated walkway
  - a mobile stair or ARGOS-type gangway for transit calls
  - 2-storey terminal building
  - baggage hall with conveyors and baggage carts/forklifts
  - provisioning truck dock and pallet area on the apron
  - shore-power cable crane or box and potable water hydrants
  - waste skips
  - coach bays (saw-tooth), taxi rank, drop-off loop, multi-storey or surface car park
  - security fence and gate
  - buses, taxis, provision trucks, forklifts, a baggage tug with carts

### Gaps
- There is no published apron width figure; it is typically quoted around 20-30 m, but unsourced here.
- I found no exact PBB tunnel widths or slopes for cruise models, and no area-per-passenger design ratio.

## Floating pontoon berths for small ferries / water taxis

### Takeaway
Small ferries use a floating pontoon (float) linked to shore by a hinged gangway, about 10-40 m long. The gangway is sized so its slope stays at 1:12 or shallower across the tidal range, with up to 1:8 allowed outside the design range. Cross slope is no more than 1:48.

### Cited Findings
- US Access Board passenger-vessel advisory committee: gangways may exceed 30 ft (9.1 m) between level landings if the slope at mean low and mean high water meets the limits. Outside the design range, the slope may not exceed 1:8 (12.5%), and cross slope may not exceed 1:48. — [US Access Board PVAAC ch.2](https://www.access-board.gov/advisory-committee-reports/passenger-vessels/pvaac-report-ch02/)
- Sausalito ferry terminal study: all slopes are capped at 1:12, which gives an 18 ft (5.5 m) gangplank for an 18 in rise. About 21 ft (6.4 m) at the float's forward end is reserved for the gangway support frame and fixed landing. — [Sausalito.gov ferry terminal study](https://sausalito.gov/home/showpublisheddocument/17791/636326254065130000)
- Observed ferry gangway lengths at US terminals range from about 35 to 130 ft (10.7-40 m), with slopes calculated at low and high tide. — [National Academies: Quantitative Procedures for Ferry Services (2023)](https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/26748/chapter/12)
- Marina guidance: a maximum 1:12 (8.33%) slope, but gangways need not be longer than 80 ft (24.4 m). — [CORADA: Accessible boating facilities, gangways](https://www.corada.com/documents/accessible-boating-facilities/gangways)

### Inferences
- BUILD LIST, pontoon:
  - rectangular pontoon about 20-30 m x 6-10 m (my estimate) with fenders and cleats
  - 2 guide piles with pile hoops, so the pontoon rises with the tide
  - a hinged aluminium gangway about 15-25 m long with handrails, a roller end and a transition plate
  - a shore abutment platform
  - a waiting shelter and ticket machine
  - a water taxi and a small ferry
- Animate the gangway angle with the tide.

### Gaps
- The Queensland TMR pontoon design criteria values (freeboard, dimensions) were not retrieved; only the table of contents was seen. — [TMR design criteria floating walkways/pontoons](https://www.tmr.qld.gov.au/-/media/busind/techstdpubs/Bridges-marine-and-other-structures/Design-criteria-Marine/DesignCriteriaFloatWalksPontoons.pdf?la=en)
- I found no source for typical pontoon sizes for water taxis.
